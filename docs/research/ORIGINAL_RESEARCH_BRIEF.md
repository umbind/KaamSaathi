**Role:**
Act as a senior India-focused product strategist, social-impact researcher, market analyst, and Android/web platform consultant.

## Objective

Conduct detailed research to identify common problems faced by **low-income and lower-middle-income Indian households** that could be solved, simplified, or made more affordable through an **Android application, mobile-friendly website, or a combination of both**.

The goal is to discover practical digital-product opportunities that:

* Solve a frequent and meaningful problem.
* Are usable by people with limited digital literacy.
* Work on low-cost Android phones.
* Can operate in low-connectivity environments.
* Support Indian languages.
* Build trust between users and local service providers.
* Have a realistic adoption and sustainability model.
* Provide economic or social value to both families and informal-sector workers.

Do not limit the research to obvious marketplace applications. Explore multiple categories of household and community problems.

## Core Research Questions

For every problem identified, answer:

* Who experiences the problem?
* How frequently does it occur?
* How serious is it?
* How do people currently solve it?
* What does the current process cost in money, time, travel, or stress?
* Why are existing solutions insufficient?
* Is the problem caused by lack of information, trust, access, affordability, coordination, documentation, or another factor?
* Can an Android app or website materially improve the situation?
* Would a simpler channel such as WhatsApp, voice calling, IVR, SMS, or assisted onboarding be needed?
* Who would pay for the service, if anyone?
* What would motivate users and providers to adopt it?
* What could prevent adoption?
* Are there legal, safety, privacy, fraud, or regulatory risks?
* Does a similar product already exist in India?
* What opportunity remains despite existing competitors?

## Important Product Constraints

Evaluate every idea against real Indian usage conditions:

* Low-cost Android devices
* Limited storage and memory
* Slow or unstable internet
* Prepaid mobile-data usage
* Limited English proficiency
* Low digital literacy
* Voice-first or icon-based interfaces
* Regional-language requirements
* Shared family smartphones
* Limited willingness to complete long registration forms
* Low trust in unknown online platforms
* Preference for calls and WhatsApp over in-app chat
* Cash and UPI payment preferences
* Difficulty uploading documents
* Aadhaar and identity-related privacy concerns
* Need for offline or low-data functionality
* Gender-related access barriers
* Accessibility for older and less-literate users

Do not recommend feature-heavy applications that would be difficult for the target users to understand or adopt.

## Evaluation Framework

Identify at least **25 distinct problem opportunities**. Score each opportunity from 1 to 10 using the following criteria:

* Severity of the problem
* Number of potential users
* Frequency of the problem
* Current level of inconvenience
* Affordability of the proposed solution
* Ease of Android/web implementation
* Ease of user adoption
* Revenue or sustainability potential
* Positive social impact
* Competitive intensity
* Trust and safety complexity
* Regulatory complexity
* Ability to launch in one city or district
* Potential to scale across India

For competitive intensity, trust complexity, and regulatory complexity, explain whether a higher score represents a more favourable or less favourable situation.

Create a weighted opportunity score and explain the weighting methodology.

## Quality Standards

* Be India-specific rather than generic.
* Focus on practical, recurring, and expensive problems.
* Do not assume every problem needs an app.
* Mention when a call centre, WhatsApp workflow, offline agent, or assisted-service model would work better.
* Avoid superficial ideas such as “create an education app” without defining the exact problem and user.
* Do not equate a large population with a viable market.
* Consider both user needs and operational feasibility.
* Account for fraud, trust, privacy, support, and last-mile execution.
* Prefer narrow, testable use cases over broad “super apps.”
* Highlight ideas that can first be validated manually.
* Clearly state uncertainties and research limitations.

## Writing Style

Write in clear, practical English suitable for a founder or product team. Explain India-specific terms where necessary. Use tables, rankings, and concise analysis instead of long generic paragraphs.

End the report with a **90-day action plan** for validating and launching the highest-ranked opportunity.
